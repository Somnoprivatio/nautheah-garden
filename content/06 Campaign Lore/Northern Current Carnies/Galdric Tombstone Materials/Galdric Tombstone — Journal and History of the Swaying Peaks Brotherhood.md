# Galdric Tombstone — Journal and History of the Swaying Peaks Brotherhood

[[06 Campaign Lore/Northern Current Carnies/Party-Known Discoveries#Galdric Tombstone and the Swaying Peaks|Galdric Tombstone]] · [[01 World/Prominent Regions/Mount Neveah/Swaying Peaks Brotherhood/Floating Home Temple|Swaying Peaks Brotherhood]] · [[04 Lore/Faiths of Nautheah#Hanseath|Hanseath]]

> [!info] Provenance
> This is a reconstruction of the damaged journal, the glowing Brotherhood history tome, and the surviving notes recovered from Galdric Tombstone's pack. It is an in-world record intended for the crew. The journal is personal testimony; the Brotherhood history is devotional tradition rather than a neutral chronicle.

## The Brotherhood in brief

The Swaying Peaks Brotherhood is a mountain order devoted to balance, fellowship, and the joyful discipline of the **Drunken Fist**. Its monks train to move like a drinker on a pitching deck: never rigid, never careless, always ready to turn a stumble into a step. The order's home temple is a floating sanctuary among the shifting heights of [[01 World/Atlas/Places/Mount Neveah|Mount Neveah]]. The monastery travels with the peaks rather than attempting to pin them in place.

The Brotherhood teaches that a life worth living must be **shared, remembered, and returned from**. A hard journey is not made holy by suffering alone. It becomes holy when the traveler brings someone else safely home, tells the truth of what happened, and raises a cup to those who did not return.

Its members are monks, brewers, archivists, couriers, cooks, and keepers of stories. The order's libraries are not silent vaults. A history is read aloud over a meal, argued over by people who were there, and tested against the scars carried by the living. Even a recipe can be an archive: the grain, water, yeast, wood, and time preserve the memory of a place.

## Hanseath and the First Pour

The Brotherhood worships [[04 Lore/Faiths of Nautheah#Hanseath|Hanseath]], whom its members call the **Laughing Brewer**, the **Keg-Bearer**, and the **Friend at the Long Table**. To the Brotherhood, Hanseath is not an excuse for foolishness or drunken cruelty. He is the sacred permission to celebrate survival honestly, to mourn without shame, and to meet danger with enough courage to keep moving.

Their central prayer is called the **First Pour**:

> *To the grain that became bread, to the fire that became warmth, to the road that became a story: pour first for what carried us, pour second for those beside us, and pour last for those who are not here to drink.*

The first cup is offered to the work that made the gathering possible. The second is shared among companions. The final cup is lifted toward the absent. A monk who drinks alone is expected to leave a portion untouched until someone else can be remembered into the room.

Hanseath's blessing is understood as a relationship rather than a transaction. The Brotherhood does not pray for endless good fortune. It asks for a steady hand, a generous table, a truthful memory, and the strength to laugh again after grief. When the order brews a historical ale, the age of the cask matters because time is part of the offering: the drink must survive long enough to become a witness.

## A short history of the Swaying Peaks Brotherhood

The oldest surviving history tome says the order began when a handful of travelers sheltered on a rock that moved with the mountain wind. They had one keg, three cracked cups, and no agreement about which way to descend. The first monk supposedly stood, swayed with the stone, and poured the last drink without spilling it. Whether the account is literal or ceremonial, the Brotherhood treats it as its founding image: **the world moves; the cup remains generous**.

Over generations the order developed four duties:

1. **The Open Hand:** protect travelers, refugees, and mountain communities without demanding obedience in return.
2. **The Moving Foot:** train the body to adapt to unstable ground, uncertain weather, and the sudden violence of the wild.
3. **The Kept Cask:** preserve food, water, brewing knowledge, and the memories attached to them.
4. **The Spoken Name:** record the dead accurately so that no one is erased by distance, shame, or a convenient lie.

The floating home temple houses the order's training rings, kitchens, fermentation cellars, and archive chambers. A small shrine to Hanseath stands beside the archive rather than in a separate sanctum. The placement is deliberate: a story is not complete until it has been shared, and a celebration is empty if it has forgotten what came before.

### Roles within the order

- **Drunken Fist monks** practice the Brotherhood's martial tradition and escort pilgrims, brewers, and couriers.
- **Cellar stewards** maintain the casks, cultures, water sources, and records of each brew.
- **Keg-bearers** carry a communal ale on long journeys; the keg is treated as a traveling shrine and emergency ration.
- **Librarians** preserve maps, songs, brewing formulas, eyewitness accounts, and the names of the dead. Galdric Tombstone served in this role.
- **Table-keepers** prepare communal meals and ensure that no guest is excluded from the circle without cause.

## Galdric Tombstone, librarian of the Brotherhood

Galdric's journal is written in a practical hand with frequent additions in the margins. He records the smell of caves, the quality of water, the names of people who offered help, and the exact point at which fear became useful. He is less interested in heroic poses than in what a group did after the danger passed: who checked the injured, who shared the last drink, and who stayed awake so someone else could sleep.

The first pages describe his appointment as a librarian. He writes that a librarian's duty is not to know every answer. It is to keep the question alive long enough for the right witness to be heard. He keeps two copies of important records: one for the archive and one for the road. The road copy is allowed to become stained, torn, and corrected. A clean history, he notes, is usually a history no one has lived.

## The Nocte Equi Trojani journey

### Leaving Lakeland City

Galdric's earliest major expedition began in [[01 World/Nations/Lakeland/Lakeland|Lakeland City]] with the group known as **Nocte Equi Trojani**. His journal calls the company “a night-riding collection of stubborn souls who had not yet learned how loudly they announced themselves.” He traveled with them as a Brotherhood librarian and field recorder, carrying a travel keg, waterproof folios, and enough charcoal to mark a route through stone.

The party entered the Underdark looking for a passage through the deep roads. Galdric's notes emphasize that the first day was not defined by monsters. It was defined by silence, bad footing, and the realization that every pool might have a ceiling, a current, or a mouth beneath it. He began a separate index of underground waters, marking which tasted of minerals, which carried spores, and which seemed to remember the surface.

### The purple worm and her children

The expedition eventually crossed the territory of a mature purple worm and its brood. Galdric describes the mother as a moving fault line: stone flexed before she appeared, and the tunnels answered her with a low, rolling thunder. The younger worms were smaller but more dangerous in a confined passage because they struck from several directions and drove the travelers toward the mother's path.

The Nocte Equi Trojani survived by refusing to fight the Underdark on its own terms. They broke line of sight, used changes in elevation, baited the brood away from the weakest travelers, and moved whenever the stone began to hum. Galdric records the purple worm's venom as a fact of natural history rather than a trophy. He notes that its bitterness remains in the mouth long after the danger has passed, and that a brewer who mistakes potency for flavor will poison a table before they impress it.

He also records the first of many rules that later appear in his alchemical notes: **never harvest a living creature while it is still defending its young**. To do so creates a bad ingredient and a worse memory.

### Nimble and the traveling drow

After the worm encounter, the company met a young gnome named **Nimble** and a traveling band of drow. The drow were not raiders. They were fugitives, guides, and liberators moving between hidden communities. They helped free drow who were still caught in [[04 Lore/Faiths of Nautheah#Lolth|Lolth]]'s clutches, breaking chains, carrying food, and guiding families through passages where a single wrong turn could bring a patrol down upon them.

Galdric writes that Nimble's name was not a boast but a promise: he was always the first person to notice the small movement that meant the group had been seen. The drow band taught the travelers that liberation in the Underdark was rarely a single battle. It was a sequence of doors opened, names remembered, and people given enough time to believe that escape could become a life.

The Brotherhood history tome treats this meeting as a lesson in Hanseath's hospitality. A table is not righteous because everyone seated at it is alike. It is righteous when the frightened are given a place before they are asked to explain why they deserve one.

### Mount Neveah and the clearing of the mists

The journey eventually brought Galdric and the Nocte Equi Trojani to [[01 World/Atlas/Places/Mount Neveah|Mount Neveah]]. He arrived while the mountain was still hidden beneath heavy mists. The fog was not ordinary weather. It muffled distance, bent familiar paths, and made every light appear farther away than it was.

Galdric's journal records the moment the mists cleared as a communal event rather than a single victory. The air changed first. Then the peaks appeared one behind another, as if the mountain were remembering its own shape. Water began to run where old channels had been dry. Bells and hammers answered from settlements that had believed themselves isolated. The Brotherhood called the event the **Rebirth of Mount Neveah** because the land did not merely become visible; it became navigable again.

He writes that the Nocte Equi Trojani stood together to watch the first clear sunrise strike the high ridges. He does not claim they caused the rebirth. He records only that they were present, that people began moving again, and that the first public toast was poured for everyone who had kept a lamp lit while the mountain was lost.

## Later pages and the final road

After Mount Neveah, Galdric returned to the Brotherhood's archives, where he compared his notes with older maps and helped establish a living record of the cleared passes. He continued to travel when the archive needed a witness rather than a copyist. His later pages describe strange Underdark fauna, unfamiliar venoms, and the difficulty of preserving a truthful account when fear changes the order of events.

The final legible entries become shorter. He writes of drow movement in the dark, of a sickness that resisted the remedies he carried, and of a deep road where the stone seemed to listen. His last complete sentence reads:

> *If the road takes my name, let it keep the parts that were useful. Let the rest be poured out for the people who found me.*

The crew encountered Galdric exhausted and gravely injured on an Underdark shore. He asked for ale, not because he believed the drink could cure him, but because he wanted his final moments to be shared. The crew honored that request. He died in revelry, with a cup raised to Hanseath and companions around him.

## What the recovered record establishes

- The Swaying Peaks Brotherhood is a living order of Drunken Fist monks, brewers, table-keepers, and librarians.
- Hanseath is central to the Brotherhood's practice of shared celebration, honest mourning, and preserved memory.
- Galdric served as a Brotherhood librarian and field recorder.
- Galdric traveled with Nocte Equi Trojani from Lakeland City through the Underdark.
- The journey included a purple worm and its brood, Nimble and a traveling drow liberation band, and the clearing of the mists and rebirth of Mount Neveah.
- Galdric's final encounter with the Drowning Promise ended in a shared reveler's feast and his death.
