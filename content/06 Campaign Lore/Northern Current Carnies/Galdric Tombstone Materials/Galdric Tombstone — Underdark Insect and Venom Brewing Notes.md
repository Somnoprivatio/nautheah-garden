# Galdric Tombstone — Underdark Insect and Venom Brewing Notes

[[06 Campaign Lore/Northern Current Carnies/Party-Known Discoveries#Galdric Tombstone and the Swaying Peaks|Galdric Tombstone]] · [[06 Campaign Lore/Northern Current Carnies/Galdric Tombstone Materials/Galdric Tombstone — Reveler's Feast|Reveler's Feast]] · [[01 World/Prominent Regions/Mount Neveah/Swaying Peaks Brotherhood/Floating Home Temple|Swaying Peaks Brotherhood]]

> [!warning] Safety and scope
> These are field notes, not permission to drink raw venom. Galdric records how fluids, glands, shed material, and trace toxins can be rendered harmless enough to influence flavor, aroma, color, fermentation, or the story carried by an ale. Every venom must be neutralized, diluted, or transformed before it comes near a serving vessel. A failed preparation is a poison, not an exotic vintage.

## Galdric's first rule: the creature is not an ingredient

Galdric begins his notes with a correction to an unnamed junior brewer: *“Do not write ‘spider’ where you mean ‘spider venom,’ and do not write ‘venom’ where you mean ‘a drop of venom after three separate changes of state.’”* The Brotherhood's brewers distinguish the living creature from the usable trace left behind by a creature.

The order prefers shed material, naturally discarded secretions, old venom sacs, molted chitin, and residue gathered after a creature has moved on. Killing an animal solely for a novelty drink violates the Brotherhood's teaching that survival creates responsibility. Galdric's best samples came from abandoned lairs, old molts, or trades with creatures that understood what he was trying to preserve.

### The neutralization ladder

1. **Separate:** remove shell, hair, grit, and living tissue from the useful fluid.
2. **Stabilize:** bind the active compounds in salt, charcoal, honey, fat, or a sacrificial ferment.
3. **Transform:** heat, age, ferment, or distill until the dangerous action has changed.
4. **Dilute:** introduce only a measured trace into a full cask.
5. **Test:** taste, smell, observe the color, and test on inert yeast before any person drinks it.
6. **Record:** note the creature, location, age of the sample, and who handled it.

## Arthropod and insect entries

### Fire beetles

**Field fact:** Fire beetles carry luminous glands that continue to glow after separation. Their light is steady rather than flickering and is strongest in newly shed or freshly deceased specimens.

**Ale use:** The gland fluid is not a fermentable ingredient. A pinprick suspended in clear spirit can create a faint ember-gold glow in a bottle or cask. Galdric used it to mark archive samples, not to make the ale itself luminous. If introduced directly to a living ferment, the light becomes cloudy and the yeast weakens.

**Flavor and effect:** No meaningful flavor when properly isolated; the visual effect resembles candlelight trapped in the drink.

**Hazard:** Never crush the gland into the wort. The concentrated fluid can irritate the throat and may cause the brew to flash or smoke when opened.

### Cave crickets and echo crickets

**Field fact:** Underdark crickets navigate by vibration and can distinguish a living footstep from a falling pebble. Their chirps become irregular around faerzress and other magical disturbances.

**Ale use:** Shed leg fibers and the thin wax from their nesting chambers can be steeped into a bittering tincture. The result carries a dry, high note that makes a drinker more aware of small sounds without granting supernatural hearing.

**Flavor and effect:** Dry grain, toasted seed, and a faint mineral snap. The effect is primarily sensory and fades quickly.

**Hazard:** Too much wax creates a numbing film on the tongue. Galdric recommends using the sound of the living colony as the “ingredient” whenever possible: brew near the nest, then move the cask away before fermentation finishes.

### Giant stag beetles

**Field fact:** Their polished chitin resists abrasion, and the adults can survive in air so stale that surface insects would suffocate. Their shells often carry spores, mineral dust, and traces of the caves they crossed.

**Ale use:** Cleanly shed shell can be used as a filtration medium. Galdric crushed only the inner, pale layers, passed the ferment through them, and removed the powder before bottling. The process clarifies a dark ale without stripping its bitterness.

**Flavor and effect:** A cleaner finish, with faint roasted-nut notes from the shell's mineral residue.

**Hazard:** Shell gathered from a corpse may contain rot, fungus, or a creature's last meal. Boil, dry, and inspect it before use.

### Giant centipedes

**Field fact:** Centipede venom is a fast-acting neurotoxin designed to stop small prey. The venom sacs remain active after death if they are kept cool.

**Ale use:** Galdric does not recommend using the venom in a beverage. He records a safer alternative: a single cleaned fang may be used to score a cask's wax seal, leaving a distinctive mark for Brotherhood inspectors. A venom that has been exhaustively neutralized can be used in microscopic quantities to create a sharp, peppery warmth, but only as an alchemical experiment.

**Flavor and effect:** Pepper, green wood, and a brief prickling sensation when properly transformed.

**Hazard:** A failed neutralization can produce numb lips, a locked jaw, or loss of breath. This entry is marked **archive only — no tavern service**.

### Giant scorpions

**Field fact:** Scorpion venom varies by age, diet, and the mineral content of the territory. A pale cave scorpion may produce a different toxin from a black scorpion found near a hot fissure.

**Ale use:** The venom is never added directly. Galdric describes using the emptied sac as a symbolic bittering pouch: it is washed, dried, filled with a non-toxic bitter herb, and hung in the fermenting cask for a measured period. The pouch gives the brewer a controlled bitterness without exposing the drink to active toxin.

**Flavor and effect:** Very bitter, smoky, and medicinal. The finished ale is associated with endurance and watchfulness.

**Hazard:** Treat every sac as live until proven otherwise. Do not assume that a dry sac is harmless.

### Giant spiders and cave spiders

**Field fact:** Spider venom is a mixture rather than a single substance. Some strains numb muscle, some slow the heart, and some disrupt a creature's sense of distance. Web silk also carries the scent of the spider's territory.

**Ale use:** Shed webbing can be rinsed, dried, and used to suspend aromatic herbs in a cask without letting them break apart. A few drops of fully neutralized venom may be used by alchemists to create a bitter, cooling cordial, but Galdric refuses to call that cordial ale.

**Flavor and effect:** Web-infused beer takes on a dry, silken mouthfeel and a faint resinous smell. The venom-derived cordial is cold and metallic.

**Hazard:** Never harvest from an active nest without a second watcher. Spider silk can conceal eggs, and a brewer who mistakes egg casing for clean webbing may bring a colony into the cellar.

### Phase spiders

**Field fact:** Phase spider venom is especially dangerous because the creature's body is not always in the same place as its wound. The venom can remain active in a sealed vessel, and a sample may appear to vanish when exposed to magical distortion.

**Ale use:** None for ordinary brewing. Galdric records a possible use for a single drop in a sealed divination flask, where it creates a brief “between places” shimmer. He underlines the note twice and adds: *“Not for drinking. Not for tasting. Not for a dare.”*

**Hazard:** Any brew exposed to phase spider venom is to be discarded and the cask burned or buried. The note is included for completeness, not recommendation.

### Carrion crawlers

**Field fact:** Carrion crawler mucus paralyzes prey and helps the creature cling to stone. The mucus remains slippery for a long time and can contaminate a large surface from a very small amount.

**Ale use:** Neutralized mucus can be used as a clarifying aid in a sealed filter, then removed with the spent grain. Galdric notes that the process can pull sediment from a harsh brew, but the margin warns that a brewer may polish away too much character.

**Flavor and effect:** Clearer color, smoother mouthfeel, and less sediment. No intended intoxicating effect beyond the ale itself.

**Hazard:** Never allow raw mucus into a serving vessel. Do not handle it without gloves, tongs, and a second clean container.

### Cave fishers

**Field fact:** Cave fishers produce adhesive filaments strong enough to pull prey from ledges. Their blood and silk carry the mineral scent of the cavern where they live.

**Ale use:** Cleaned, dried filament can be used to suspend botanicals in a cask. It is removed before bottling. A small amount of blood, reduced and filtered, can add a dark red-brown hue, but Galdric prefers mineral pigments for color and lists blood only as a last resort.

**Flavor and effect:** A faint mineral smell and a heavier body when the silk is used as a filter support.

**Hazard:** The adhesive remains active until thoroughly washed. Do not allow it to contact a bung, cork, or brewer's eyelids.

### Ettercaps and spider-changed kin

**Field fact:** Ettercap silk is often chemically altered by the creature's diet and may contain traces of venom, fungus, or carrion. It should not be treated as ordinary spider silk.

**Ale use:** The silk can be used as a coarse strainer only after repeated washing and a full day of drying. Galdric records that the best use is symbolic: a “web-cleared” ale served to travelers who escaped a trap.

**Flavor and effect:** No intended flavor. The name of the process is more important than the material.

**Hazard:** The silk may carry enchantment, disease, or eggs. If it twitches, discard it.

### Stirges

**Field fact:** Stirges feed on blood and can carry disease from one host to another. Their bodies are fragile but their feeding tubes are resilient.

**Ale use:** None. Galdric lists stirge blood only as a warning example under the heading **“A fluid is not a flavor.”**

**Hazard:** Any beverage touched by stirge blood is destroyed. The brewer scrubs the entire work surface and begins again.

### Rust monsters

**Field fact:** Rust monsters carry a reactive digestive fluid that breaks down iron and other ferrous metals. The fluid is not a poison in the conventional sense; it is a solvent with a creature's instincts behind it.

**Ale use:** Never add the fluid to a drink. A trace diluted in vinegar can remove iron stains from brewing tools, after which the tools must be rinsed and tested. Galdric also uses a rust monster's shed antenna tip as a warning marker on dangerous casks.

**Flavor and effect:** None intended.

**Hazard:** A single unnoticed drop can ruin iron hoops, nails, knives, or a brewing stand.

### Umber hulks

**Field fact:** Umber hulks are not insects, but their chitin, mandibles, and earth-scented fluids appear in Galdric's Underdark index because they share the hazards of large burrowing arthropods. Their gaze and tunneling behavior make them especially dangerous around cellars.

**Ale use:** Shed chitin may be carved into a cask token or used as a heat-resistant stirring paddle. Galdric rejects the use of umber hulk blood, bile, or brain matter in any drink. The notes state that their fluids can carry disorienting compounds and that the safest alchemical use is external: marking a tunnel or testing whether a wall has recently been disturbed.

**Flavor and effect:** No safe beverage application recorded.

**Hazard:** Severe confusion, vertigo, and uncontrolled aggression are possible. This entry is a warning, not a recipe.

## Larger Underdark creatures with brewing-relevant fluids

### Purple worms

**Field fact:** Purple worm venom is bitter enough to linger after neutralization, while the creature's digestive fluids carry the mineral taste of the tunnels it consumes. Galdric encountered a mother and brood during the Nocte Equi Trojani journey and records the danger with unusual care.

**Ale use:** Only the old, inert residue from a shed tooth or abandoned burrow may be studied. A properly treated trace can sharpen bitterness and create a long mineral finish. It should never be harvested from a living worm or used as a direct additive.

**Flavor and effect:** Stone, bitter root, and a dry heat at the back of the throat.

**Hazard:** A failed treatment can cause swelling, loss of sensation, and organ failure. Galdric marks purple-worm material as **memorial ingredient only**: it is acceptable to record, not necessary to brew.

### Darkmantles

**Field fact:** Darkmantle mucus helps the creature cling to ceilings and can interfere with light. Its membrane retains a cold, damp scent long after it dries.

**Ale use:** Washed membrane can be used to cover a fermentation jar during transport, keeping out dust and surface spores. It is removed before the vessel is opened.

**Flavor and effect:** No intended flavor; a covered ferment may develop a softer, less oxidized aroma.

**Hazard:** Raw mucus can foul a batch and extinguish lamps nearby.

### Hook horrors

**Field fact:** Hook horrors leave oily traces on stone and wood from their claws and feathers. The oil is not venom, but it carries a strong animal scent and can turn rancid.

**Ale use:** After rendering and repeated filtration, a trace can be used to smoke the outside of a cask, imparting a roasted, gamey aroma without touching the drink. Galdric prefers to imitate the scent with charred grain.

**Hazard:** Spoiled oil contaminates wood and attracts scavengers.

## Fungal, mineral, and insect-adjacent brewing companions

Galdric's notes cross-reference the creatures with the safer ingredients already known to Underdark brewers:

- **Caveworm honey:** a fermentable sugar and preservative gathered from luminous worm colonies; it lends a gentle sweetness that can soften bitter historical ales.
- **Underglow moss:** a mild glowing ferment that adds a blue-green sheen when used sparingly.
- **Darkspore mushrooms:** earthy fermentable material with a dark, nutty flavor.
- **Firecap extract:** a spicy accent best used in drops rather than spoonfuls.
- **Ghostroot essence:** a cool, mint-like counterpoint to venom-derived bitterness.
- **Myconid dust:** a shimmering, umami-rich addition only when collected with the consent of the myconid community.
- **Glowstone powder:** a fermentation catalyst and visual marker; too much produces a chalky finish.
- **Faerzress infusion:** a magical enhancer that must be treated as unstable and never used without a trained alchemist.

## Final margin: what Galdric wanted remembered

The last page is not a recipe. It is an instruction to future librarians:

> *Write down what the creature was doing before you took anything from it. Write down whether it had a choice. Write down who drank the result and whether they were told the truth. If the drink is remarkable but the story is cruel, keep the story and throw away the drink.*

The Brotherhood's brewing tradition is therefore less about making dangerous ales and more about learning where danger ends. The best bottle in Galdric's archive is not the one with the rarest venom. It is the one whose ingredients were gathered without needless harm, whose preparation was understood, and whose history can be told without shame.
