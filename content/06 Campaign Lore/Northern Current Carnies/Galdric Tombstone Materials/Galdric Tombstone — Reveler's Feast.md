# Galdric Tombstone — Reveler's Feast

[[06 Campaign Lore/Northern Current Carnies/Party-Known Discoveries#Galdric Tombstone and the Swaying Peaks|Galdric Tombstone]] · [[04 Lore/Faiths of Nautheah#Hanseath|Hanseath]] · [[01 World/Prominent Regions/Mount Neveah/Swaying Peaks Brotherhood/Floating Home Temple|Swaying Peaks Brotherhood]]

> [!info] The recovered recipe book
> Galdric's book calls this a **three-course Reveler's Feast**: one ale for each course, each one a historical brew aged for years and served with a story. Marginalia references the Brotherhood's longer five-course ceremonial service, but Galdric chose the shorter three-pour form for a traveling feast. The final lunch course is missing from the recovered book.

## The meaning of the feast

The Brotherhood does not treat a historical ale as a luxury drink. It is a liquid archive. Grain remembers the soil, water remembers the stone, yeast remembers the hands that kept it alive, and the cask remembers every season spent in the dark. A brewer who serves an old ale is expected to tell the truth about what it preserves.

The feast begins with the words **“For what carried us,”** continues with **“For those beside us,”** and ends with **“For those who are not here.”** The first pour belongs to labor and endurance. The second belongs to fellowship. The last belongs to memory. When the final course is missing, the table is considered incomplete rather than ruined; the absence itself becomes part of the record.

## Course One — The Nail That Holds the Sky

**Service:** First pour, taken before food. The cup is held in both hands and offered briefly toward the nearest beam, mast, or mountain wall.

**Age:** Eleven years in a narrow ironwood cask, then transferred to black ceramic for three winters.

**Appearance:** Nearly black at the center, with a thin copper rim when held to light. The foam collapses quickly into a dark cream line.

**Flavor:** Very bitter, dry, and smoky. The first taste is burnt grain and iron. The second reveals spruce resin, roasted root, and a sharp mineral finish. It is intentionally not sweet. The bitterness is meant to recall the cost of building a home where the world refuses to stay still.

**Historical memory:** The ale commemorates the first anchoring of the Swaying Peaks Brotherhood's floating home temple. The story says the monks could not stop the rock from moving, so they learned to build in a way that allowed movement. Every nail, rope, and joint became part of the temple's prayer.

**Galdric's note:**

> *Do not apologize for this cup. It is bitter because the mountain was bitter. A drink that becomes pleasant only after the third swallow is a fair portrait of any honest life.*

**Brewing notes:** The bittering herbs are added in three intervals rather than at once. A small amount of mineral-rich spring water is used to sharpen the finish. The cask must be opened during a storm, not because thunder improves the drink, but because the brewer must remember that a roof is a temporary agreement with the sky.

## Course Two — Mists Unbound

**Service:** Second pour, served after the first shared dish. The brewer pours for every person at the table, including absent companions whose names are spoken aloud.

**Age:** Thirty-seven years in a high-altitude cask, with a further seven years of bottle aging. The oldest surviving bottle was reserved for Galdric's personal archive.

**Appearance:** Burnished amber beneath a veil of silver-gray haze. When poured, a faint mist rolls over the surface and settles only after the first toast.

**Flavor:** Bitter orange peel, charred honey, cold stone, and mountain herbs. The finish is long and austere, with a faint sweetness that appears only after the drink is swallowed. The ale warms the chest but leaves the tongue cool.

**Historical memory:** This is the ale of the journey with **Nocte Equi Trojani**. It remembers the Underdark route from [[01 World/Nations/Lakeland/Lakeland|Lakeland City]], the purple worm and her children, the young gnome Nimble, and the traveling drow who helped free their kin from [[04 Lore/Faiths of Nautheah#Lolth|Lolth]]'s clutches. Its final scent is intended to recall the first clear wind that moved across [[01 World/Atlas/Places/Mount Neveah|Mount Neveah]] after the mists lifted.

**Galdric's note:**

> *The mist did not vanish. It was persuaded to move. Remember that distinction. A mountain can be hidden and still be alive beneath the veil.*

**Brewing notes:** The base malt is dried in clean mountain air and never over-roasted. The bittering herbs are steeped after fermentation so they remain bright rather than becoming heavy. A single spoon of water from a clear highland channel is added at bottling. The recipe warns against using water that has passed through a place where a name has been forgotten.

## Course Three — The Last Lunch / The Unwritten Chapter

**Status:** Missing.

The third course was intended for the midday table, after the historical toasts and before the final meal. The recipe book contains a torn gathering list, a blank line in Galdric's hand, and the edge of a page marked with a partial cask symbol. The title is gone. The serving order survives only as a marginal instruction:

> *Bring it after the names have been spoken. Do not sweeten it to spare them. Let the last cup taste of the road.*

The missing course was likely a highly personalized brew prepared for Galdric's own return from the Underdark. It was to be bitter, strongly aged, and poured only after the guests had heard the full account of the journey. The final line of the surviving page reads:

> *If I am absent, pour the first mouthful into the earth and the second into the hands of whoever stayed.*

No surviving page identifies the ingredients, the cask's location, or whether the ale was ever finished. The absence leaves several possibilities open:

- Galdric had not yet brewed it and meant to complete it after returning to the Brotherhood.
- The recipe page was removed deliberately because the final ale was intended for a private memorial.
- The last lunch course was brewed from an ingredient that could not safely be named in an ordinary archive.
- Galdric carried the missing bottle separately and lost it before reaching the Drowning Promise.

## Optional five-course Brotherhood service

The marginalia also describes the Brotherhood's full ceremonial table. It is not the feast Galdric prepared for his final night, but it explains the phrase **five-course service** found in the archive:

1. **The First Pour:** a bitter ale for labor and shelter.
2. **The Shared Cup:** a communal ale for companions and guests.
3. **The Road Course:** a historical ale for the journey that brought the table together.
4. **The Quiet Course:** a small, low-alcohol pour for the names of the dead.
5. **The Last Lunch:** the personal ale of the host, served only when the story has been told.

Each course uses one ale, but the food between courses may be simple: dark bread, smoked roots, cave mushrooms, salted fish, and whatever the travelers can honestly spare. The drink is the archive; the meal is the proof that the archive still has people to feed.

## Service instructions for the crew

If the crew recreates the feast, the Brotherhood would expect the following order:

1. Place the glowing history tome beside the first cask or bottle.
2. Speak the names of anyone whose work made the gathering possible.
3. Pour **The Nail That Holds the Sky** and allow the bitterness to settle before eating.
4. Share **Mists Unbound** only after everyone at the table has a cup.
5. Leave the third place setting empty if the missing ale cannot be recovered.
6. Do not invent a name for the missing course. A false name would turn an absence into a lie.
7. End with the Brotherhood's final toast: **“May the road remember us kindly, and may we remember one another accurately.”**

## Brewing principles recorded in the margins

- Age is not the same as quality. An old ale that has been forgotten is only old; an old ale that has been tended becomes a witness.
- Bitterness should be shaped, not hidden. The drink should challenge the tongue without punishing the drinker.
- A historical brew must be paired with a true account. If the story changes, the recipe's notes must be amended rather than quietly corrected.
- The final cup is never poured for an empty room. Someone living must hear the toast.
- If the brewer cannot share the ale, they should preserve the ingredients and leave instructions for the next table.
